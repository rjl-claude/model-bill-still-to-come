# Hand-worked example: Hinkley Point C's two reactors (aios-harness#65)

Sources: HPC CfD (Nov 2022 consolidated), `data/raw/hpc/`; facts 1-6 in `docs/facts.md`; rulings, build plan points 13, 23, 24.

1. **Strike.** £89.50/MWh in 2012 prices (cl. 11.1; Sizewell C's licence read as "equivalent support", point 23), raised to 2024 prices by DESNZ's AR7 ratio 91.20/65.45: **£124.71/MWh**. Bracket: £92.50 -> £128.89.
2. **Capacity.** LCCC lists 3,287.16 MW for the contract: **1,643.58 MW per reactor**.
3. **Starts (central).** Reactor One 1 April 2030 (EDF: first power in 2030; April as the OBR's forecast first pays it in 2030-31); Reactor Two a year later, 1 April 2031.
4. **Ends.** Each runs to the 35th anniversary of the *earlier* of its start and the last day of its target commissioning window (cl. 5). Reactor One's window runs from 1 May 2025 to but excluding 1 May 2029, so its last day is 30 April 2029: **ends 30 April 2064**. Reactor Two: 1 Nov 2025 to but excluding 1 Nov 2029, last day 31 October 2029: **ends 31 October 2064**. So Reactor One is paid for 34.1 years, Reactor Two for 33.6.
5. **Bracket "35 years from start":** ends 1 April 2065 and 1 April 2066.
6. **Bracket "late":** each start two years later (1 Apr 2032, 1 Apr 2033): the ends do not move (both windows have closed), so each is paid for two years fewer. "Early": each a year earlier (1 Apr 2029: before Reactor One's window closes, so its end is 1 Apr 2064; Reactor Two 1 Apr 2030, end 31 Oct 2064).
7. **Generation cap.** Payments stop when 910,000,000 MWh have been paid for, across both reactors (cl. 1.1, 6.1); applied in the CfD line (#68).
