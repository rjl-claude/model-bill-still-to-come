## Hinkley Point C (CfD, November 2022 consolidated; `hpc/hinkley-point-c-contract-for-difference-november-2022.pdf`)

1. **Strike price.** "The Initial Strike Price is £89.50/MWh (expressed in Money of the Year for the Base Year)" (cl. 11.1). Clause 14.1: if the *Sizewell C Condition* is not met on or before the Reactor One Start Date, the strike rises by £3/MWh (indexed) from that date, and falls back by £3 from the day the condition is met.
2. **The Sizewell C Condition** (cl. 1.1) is the earlier of (A) Sizewell C entering "a FiT Contract for Difference (or equivalent support)" and meeting any State aid condition, and (B) the first nuclear-island concrete pour at Sizewell C. Sizewell C took its final investment decision on 22 July 2025 under a regulated asset base licence, not a CfD (`sizewell-c/szc-fid-vfm-assessment.pdf`; licence special conditions v7, 19 Jul 2025). Whether the licence counts as "equivalent support" is not stated in any saved source. **For Richard to rule** (central £89.50 or £92.50 until first nuclear concrete).
3. **Term.** Each reactor runs to the 35th anniversary of the *earlier* of its Start Date and the last day of its Target Commissioning Window (cl. 5). Target commissioning dates: Reactor One 1 May 2025, Reactor Two 1 Nov 2025; each window is four years (cl. 2.1, 2.2), so they end 30 Apr 2029 and 31 Oct 2029, extended day for day only for the delays listed in cl. 2.3. So a reactor that starts after its window is paid for fewer than 35 years. **For Richard to rule** (whether any extension is assumed).
4. **Generation cap.** The contract expires when 910,000,000 MWh have been paid for (cl. 1.1 "Contracted Generation Cap", cl. 6.1).
5. **Market reference price** is the baseload (season-ahead) reference price (cl. 12), not the hourly day-ahead price.
6. **Start.** EDF's 2025 results (Feb 2026) put Unit 1's first power in 2030 (New Civil Engineer, 20 Feb 2026; World Nuclear News). Unit 2: not given in those reports. Open: EDF's own release, to save.

## CfD negative-price rules (standard terms, `cfd-terms/`)

8. **AR1 (Aug 2014 terms):** no negative-price rule; when the hourly reference price is negative the generator is paid strike minus a negative price. The Investment Contracts (FIDeR, 2014) are the same: the Hornsea Phase 1 Investment Contract (statement, agreement and conditions, in `model-ending-subsidy-contracts/data/raw/desnz/fider-IC_H-S.zip`) has no negative-price clause (checked 4 Nov, #67).
9. **AR2 (Mar 2017) and AR3 (2019):** for intermittent plant, no payment in any run of **six or more consecutive hours** of negative day-ahead price ("Intermittent Rolling Negative Price Period"); baseload plant, twelve or more.
10. **AR4, AR5, AR6, AR7:** no payment in **any hour** of negative price ("Intermittent Negative Price Period": one or more settlement units).

## Renewables Obligation

12. **End dates.** Stations first accredited on or before 25 June 2008 get no certificates for their original capacity after **31 March 2027**; later stations none on or after the 20th anniversary of accreditation or 31 March 2037, whichever is earlier (RO Order 2015 art. 44; ROS Order 2009 art. 17A; NIRO 2009 art. 18A; copied to `data/raw/legislation/` in #70). The build plan's "20 years or March 2037" misses the 2027 cut-off.
13. **Taxpayers' share.** 75% of the domestic share of the RO funded by the Exchequer in 2026-27, 2027-28 and 2028-29, from 1 April 2026; domestic share "approximately 41 per cent" (DESNZ); Exchequer cost £2,580m, £2,100m, £2,195m (`hmt/Budget_2025-Policy_Costings.pdf`, p.12). Budget 2026 restates it (`hmt/budget-2026.pdf`).
14. **Indexation.** The buy-out price is indexed by CPI from 1 April 2026 (was RPI): 2026-27 buy-out £69.34 (`ofgem/ro-buy-out-price-mutualisation-2026-27.html`; DESNZ decision 28 Jan 2026).

## Who pays

15. **Households' share.** DESNZ's own estimate for the RO: about 41% of RO costs levied on households (fact 13). Not the model's split: point 18 holds households' 2025 share of electricity (34.9%, fact 18), with DESNZ's projection (fact 19) as the bracket. The 41% is what the Budget's Exchequer costing uses; the gap is checked and explained (Stage 4).
16. **Manufacturers' exemption.** From 1 April 2027, eligible firms in Great Britain are exempt from the indirect costs of the RO, FiT and Capacity Market, with a back payment for 2026-27; about 10,000 firms (`hmt/budget-2026.pdf`). CfD costs are not exempted.

Costs are shown as in the Budget table; its price basis is not stated there.
21. **Forward prices 2026-27.** The market's season-ahead GB baseload price is the CfD Baseload Market Reference Price, set twice a year: **Winter 2026 (October 2026 to March 2027) £114.50/MWh**, Summer 2026 (April to September 2026) £75.30/MWh, Winter 2025 £84.75/MWh (`lccc/market-reference-prices.html`, table "season and its associated BMRP (£/MWh)"). In LCCC's words: "Baseload prices are calculated using a traded volume weighted average based on forward season data received from the London Energy Brokers' Association (LEBA). The BMRP is published in April and October of each year" (`lccc/baseload-market-reference-price-winter-2026.html`, news item, Oct 2026; the page gives no day). Cash (nominal) prices. The next one, Summer 2027, comes in April 2027. LCCC's own simulated forecast (`lccc/forecast_market_reference_prices.csv`, columns Quarterly_Obligation_Period and Forecast_Reference_Price_GBP_per_MWh, labelled "Determination 01/10/2026-31/12/2026" and "Determination 01/01/2027-31/03/2027", fetched 2 Nov 2026, no creation date in the file) covers only 1 Oct 2026 to 31 Mar 2027; its biomass-type "Forecast BMRP" rows average £108.65/MWh for Oct-Dec 2026 and £111.90/MWh for Jan-Mar 2027, below the published £114.50; its wind- and solar-weighted "Forecast IMRP" rows run £114.83 to £143.17/MWh by month. No saved source gives a market forward curve beyond March 2027. **Used** (point 26's bracket): these season-ahead prices to March 2027, then Annex M. Ofgem's wholesale market indicators page publishes its forward-price charts only as images, with no data file.
22. **Solar decline.** Jordan & Kurtz find a median of **0.5%/year** and a mean of **0.8%/year**: "Nearly 2000 degradation rates, measured on individual modules or entire systems, have been assembled from the literature, showing a median value of 0.5%/year" (`nrel/jordan-kurtz-2012-pv-degradation-rates-nrel-ja-5200-51664.pdf`, abstract, p.1); "The distribution is skewed toward high degradation rates with a mean of 0.8%/year and a median of 0.5%/year. The majority of these reported rates, 78% of all data, are below a rate of 1%/year" (section 3.1, p.5); Figure 2(a), p.6, "Median: 0.5 %/year", "Average: 0.8 %/year", "# reported rates = 1920"; the Conclusion (p.16) repeats "a mean degradation rate of 0.8%/year and a median value of 0.5%/year". Thin-film "statistically closer to 1%/year" (Conclusion). Page numbers are those of this file. The file is the NREL author preprint (NREL/JA-5200-51664, June 2012, "To be published in Progress in Photovoltaics"), saved from OSTI; the published article is Prog. Photovolt. 21 (2013) 12-29, and its page numbers differ. The text of the preprint was not compared with the published version.
23. **Capacity Market indexation.** The capacity payment price is the auction's cleared price indexed by CPI, for T-4 auctions: "If the relevant capacity auction was a T-4 auction, PE is to be calculated in accordance with the formula" PE = CCP x (CPI of the winter ending 30 April before the delivery year / CPI of the auction's base period) (`legislation/uksi-2014-2043-Electricity-Capacity-Regulations-2014-latest.pdf`, Schedule 1 (reg. 36), para 3(5) and 3(7), pp.89-90; the formula is in the `.xml` copy beside it). Para 3(7) defines the two CPI terms (symbols are in the `.xml`; the PDF text drops them): CPI_x "means the average of the monthly values of CPI for the months of the winter ending on the 30th April preceding the start of year X"; CPI_base "means the average of the monthly values of CPI for the months of the base period referred to in regulation 11 for the relevant capacity auction". Reg. 2(1): "'CPI' means the UK Consumer Prices Index (All Items) published monthly by the Office for National Statistics"; "'base period' means a period starting on 1st October and ending on the following 30th April, to be used in calculating capacity payments for the purpose of adjusting for inflation the capacity cleared price". The base period is fixed per auction (reg. 11(1)(f)) and recorded on the register (reg. 31(2)(e)). Para 3(6) is omitted from 23 Jul 2025 (S.I. 2025/917), and para 3(4) now points only to 3(5), so the Regulations as saved give a formula for T-4 agreements only; how T-1 agreements are priced is to be read from the Capacity Market Rules in stage 2 (#81). The text is the version in force when saved ("Document Generated: 2026-08-19"). Only the Regulations were read; the Capacity Market Rules were not.

## Stage 2 (5 Nov): FiT and Capacity Market

24. **FiT end dates.** "Most generators accredited on the FIT are eligible to receive payments for a maximum period of 20 years following their eligibility date, except for solar photovoltaic (PV) installations accredited before 1 August 2012, which have a maximum eligibility period of 25 years, and all micro-CHP installations, which have a maximum eligibility period of 10 years" (`ofgem/feed_in_tariff_annual_report_sy15.pdf`, para 2.2, p.21); other technologies have "from 17 to 25 years" and start to reach the end "from 2027 (SY17)" (para 2.17, p.31): the 17 years are the installations migrated from the RO in April 2010, so to 31 March 2027; Ofgem "will keep servicing generators up until 31 March 2042" (para 7.1).
25. **FiT measure and SY15.** Scheme value £1.84bn = generation payments £1,732,082,151 + export payments £94,088,277 + qualifying FIT costs £17,538,180 (Figs 3.5, 3.6, pp.40-41); levelisation fund £1,725,741,218 (export payments net of their value to licensees). 7.97 TWh generated, 1.47 TWh exported (para 3.6, p.34). The Clock counts the scheme value (Clock `/srv/aios/projects/web-subsidy-clock/reference/ref_totals.yaml`: "Ofgem's published FIT scheme value (generation + export + qualifying costs)").
26. **FiT output by technology.** DUKES 6.3 load factors, 2023-25 mean: solar PV 10.27%, onshore wind 24.69%, small-scale hydro 37.70%, anaerobic digestion 62.51% (`desnz/DUKES_6.3.xlsx`, sheet `6.3`, rows 6, 9, 11, 18). 27. **Capacity Market monthly weighting.** Each month's capacity payment is weighted by its share of GB demand over the previous three years (`legislation/uksi-2014-2043-Electricity-Capacity-Regulations-2014-latest.pdf`, Sch. 1 para 2, p.88). T-1 and transitional auctions have no base period (`neso/capacity-market-auction-static-data.csv`, column "Base Period for the Agreement"), so their price is not indexed (fact 23).
28. **Capacity Market net-zero share.** Clock engine v4-s43 (sha256 e1cf23af...): 2025 14.61%; 2021-25 lowest 13.60% (2022), highest 15.55% (2024) (netzero_gbp / cm_paid_gbp).
